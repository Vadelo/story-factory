# Brainstorming Questionnaire

Use this as a menu, not an interrogation. Ask the missing questions that most affect the work. If the user does not answer, suggest defaults.

## Core Brief

- What form should this be: short story, novel, saga, screenplay, comic script, webnovel, RPG campaign, children's story, interactive fiction, or something else?
- What is the seed idea in one sentence?
- What language should the output use?
- Who is the target reader or audience?
- How long should the output be now?
- Should this be a new work or continuation of an existing work corpus?

## Genre And Promise

- Primary genre and secondary genre?
- What emotional promise should the reader feel?
- What should the story not become?
- What rating or content boundary should apply?
- Any comp titles, inspirations, or anti-inspirations?

## Character

- Who is the protagonist?
- What do they want externally?
- What do they need internally?
- What flaw, fear, wound, contradiction, or secret complicates them?
- Who opposes them, and why?
- Which relationship carries the most emotional voltage?

## Plot

- What event disrupts the normal world?
- What is at stake if the protagonist fails?
- What major reveal, twist, or reversal should exist?
- Should the ending be happy, tragic, bittersweet, open, circular, or shocking?
- What is the intended pace: slow burn, escalating, episodic, propulsive, contemplative?

## World

- Where and when does this happen?
- What social, magical, technological, political, or supernatural rules matter?
- What details must remain realistic?
- What details can be invented freely?

## Style

- POV: first, close third, omniscient, multiple POV, screenplay, epistolary?
- Tense: past or present?
- Prose style: lyrical, direct, cinematic, literary, humorous, dark, sparse, ornate?
- Dialogue style?

## Defaults To Suggest

When the user leaves blanks, propose a compact brief:

```text
I suggest: <genre>, <tone>, <audience>, <POV>, <tense>, <length>, with <core conflict>.
```

Mark suggestions as provisional until accepted or contradicted.
