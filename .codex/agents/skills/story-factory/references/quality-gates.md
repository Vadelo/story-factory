# Quality Gates

## Gate 1: Brief

- Form, language, audience, length, genre, and output are known or suggested.
- Missing answers are represented as provisional suggestions.
- Sensitive boundaries are explicit.

## Gate 2: Genre Promise

- The work knows what pleasure it offers: wonder, dread, romance, suspense, comedy, catharsis, mystery, etc.
- The opening creates the correct expectation.
- The ending type matches or intentionally subverts the genre.

## Gate 3: Character

- Protagonist has desire, pressure, contradiction, and choice.
- Antagonistic force has a reason, not only a function.
- Supporting characters change the protagonist's options.

## Gate 4: Plot And Scenes

- Every major scene changes the situation.
- Stakes escalate or deepen.
- Reveals are prepared before they pay off.
- The climax forces an irreversible decision.

## Gate 5: World And Continuity

- Rules are clear enough to prevent arbitrary solutions.
- Timeline is consistent.
- Names, ages, places, powers, relationships, and injuries do not drift.
- Retcons are named explicitly.

## Gate 6: Prose

- POV and tense stay stable unless intentionally changed.
- Dialogue has subtext.
- Description supports action, emotion, or atmosphere.
- Style serves the target reader.

## Gate 7: Memory

- The work corpus is updated after major output.
- Searchable sections exist for canon, characters, timeline, style, drafts, and open threads.
- Future continuation can recover the project without relying on chat history.
